Division of Property in China After a Court Divorce Obtained by a Foreign Couple

2026-04-17

When a foreign couple (neither party holding Chinese nationality) has obtained a divorce through litigation and the court judgment — issued by a court outside China — has become final and includes a decision on the division of property located in China, the handling of that property in China cannot, as a general rule, be completed directly through the special procedure for recognition of a foreign court divorce judgment in China. This is because that special procedure does not itself cover property division. Moreover, when both parties are foreign nationals, a Chinese court will in principle decline to accept an application under that special procedure even with respect to the dissolution of the marital status.

From the perspective of the general regime for recognition and enforcement of foreign civil judgments, it is not absolutely impossible for a final foreign civil judgment to be recognized and enforced in China — Chinese courts have precedents of recognizing foreign civil judgments on the basis of reciprocity. However, with respect to cases involving the division of property located in China as part of a foreign divorce judgment, no relevant final ruling appears to have been issued, at least within the publicly searchable scope.

For these reasons, a separate action must be brought before a Chinese court for the division of property located in China.

When filing such an action in China, the cause of action should be identified as a property dispute after divorce. Regarding territorial jurisdiction: if the defendant has a domicile or habitual residence in China, the court of that domicile or habitual residence shall have jurisdiction; if the defendant has no domicile in China, jurisdiction may be determined by reference to factors such as the location of the subject matter of the litigation and the location of attachable property.

As a matter of substantive law, the governing law shall be determined in accordance with Article 24 of the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Law Applicable to Foreign-Related Civil Relations. With respect to matrimonial property relations, the parties may agree to apply the law of the habitual residence of one party, the law of the nationality of one party, or the law of the place where the principal property is located. In the absence of a choice by the parties, the law of the common habitual residence shall apply; if there is no common habitual residence, the law of the common nationality shall apply.

A prerequisite for bringing such an action is that the marriage between the parties has already been dissolved. If litigation concerning the marital relationship is still pending, the above-described action cannot be filed before a Chinese court. When filing the action, the party should submit the final divorce judgment together with its Chinese translation and other supporting materials to establish the fact of the divorce.
