China's Legal Framework for Exit Restrictions on Foreign Nationals

Under China's current laws, foreign nationals present in China may, under specified circumstances, be lawfully prohibited from leaving the country (referred to below as exit restrictions). Exit restrictions on foreign nationals are not governed by a single statute; rather, they are dispersed across multiple legal domains, including entry and exit administration, civil enforcement proceedings, supervisory investigations, criminal procedure, labour protection, and national security. Understanding this framework requires a systematic review of each legal basis, a clear delineation of the authority of each competent organ, and identification of the risk scenarios most practically relevant to foreign business personnel operating in China.

I. The Overarching Basis: Article 28 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law

Article 28 of the Law of the People's Republic of China on Exit and Entry Administration (enacted 2012, effective 1 July 2013) is the core legal basis for restricting the departure of foreign nationals. It sets out, by means of an exhaustive enumeration, four categories of persons who are not permitted to exit.

The first category concerns persons involved in criminal cases. Foreign nationals who have been sentenced to criminal punishment and whose sentence has not yet been fully served, or who are defendants or criminal suspects in criminal cases, shall not be permitted to exit. An exception applies to sentenced persons transferred pursuant to agreements concluded between China and foreign countries.

The second category concerns unresolved civil cases. Foreign nationals with pending civil cases in China, where the people's court has issued a decision, shall not be permitted to exit. This category is most commonly encountered in foreign-related commercial and civil disputes — including contract disputes, equity disputes, and other commercial matters — and may be applied at the pre-litigation preservation stage, during litigation, or at the enforcement stage.

The third category concerns unpaid wages. Foreign nationals who have failed to pay wages owed to workers, where a decision prohibiting departure has been issued by the relevant department of the State Council or by the people's government of a province, autonomous region, or municipality directly under the Central Government, shall not be permitted to exit. It should be particularly noted that the authority to issue such decisions rests exclusively with the relevant department of the State Council or a provincial-level people's government; labour dispute arbitration commissions and labour administrative authorities are not within the scope of authorised bodies and may not independently issue exit restriction decisions on this basis.

The fourth category is a catch-all provision. Other circumstances prescribed by laws or administrative regulations where a person shall not be permitted to exit require explicit authorisation from a specific law or administrative regulation and may not be invoked arbitrarily on the basis of general administrative investigations or ordinary commercial disputes.

II. Exit Restrictions in Civil Enforcement Proceedings

In enforcement proceedings, where a person subject to enforcement fails to fulfil obligations determined by legally effective instruments, the people's court may impose exit restriction measures or notify the relevant units to assist in implementing such measures. The current legal basis is Article 255 of the Civil Procedure Law of the People's Republic of China (as amended in 2021), together with Articles 23 and 24 of the Interpretation of the Supreme People's Court on Several Issues Concerning the Application of Enforcement Procedures under the Civil Procedure Law (as amended in 2020).

As regards the scope of persons subject to restriction, the judicial interpretation provides that where the person subject to enforcement is a natural person, the exit restriction measure shall target that person directly; where such person lacks or has limited civil capacity, the measure may be directed at their legal guardian. Where the person subject to enforcement is an entity, the measures may be directed at its legal representative, principal responsible persons, or directly responsible personnel whose conduct affects the fulfilment of the debt.

In practice, actual controllers, controlling shareholders, directors, and personnel who handled the specific project in dispute may all be found to constitute directly responsible personnel whose conduct affects fulfilment of the debt.

As regards initiation, exit restriction measures may be applied for in writing by the applicant for enforcement to the enforcing court, or may be decided by the enforcing court on its own initiative. The restriction period is generally three to six months; the applicant for enforcement may apply for renewal before expiry, and as long as the obligation remains unfulfilled, the restriction may in theory be continuously renewed.

It should be particularly noted that at the pre-litigation or litigation preservation stage, the court may also, upon application, issue a ruling imposing exit restrictions without waiting for a judgment to become legally effective. For foreign-related commercial disputes, the conditions are more stringent: pursuant to paragraph 50 of the 2021 Supreme People's Court Minutes of the Symposium on Foreign-Related Commercial and Maritime Trial Work, three conditions must be simultaneously satisfied — there must be an unresolved foreign-related commercial dispute; the applicant must have a relatively high likelihood of success and the respondent must present a risk of using departure to evade litigation or performance; and departure must be likely to cause the case to become difficult to try or impossible to enforce. Furthermore, where the respondent has sufficient attachable assets within the territory, exit restriction measures shall in principle not be imposed.

III. Exit Restrictions in Supervisory Investigations

Article 30 of the Law of the People's Republic of China on Supervision (effective 2018) provides that, in order to prevent persons under investigation and related persons from absconding abroad, supervisory organs may, upon approval from a supervisory organ at the provincial level or above, lawfully decide to impose exit restriction measures, which are then executed by immigration management authorities. Article 162 of the Implementing Regulations of the Law on Supervision (effective 2021) further clarifies the procedure: the supervisory organ shall issue a relevant letter and a written decision on exit restriction measures; the period of validity shall in general not exceed three months and shall lapse automatically upon expiry; continuation requires reapplication through the original procedure, with each extension not exceeding three months.

It should be noted that the above provisions apply to persons under investigation and related persons and are not limited to Chinese nationals; foreign nationals with a connection to the subject matter of a supervisory investigation may equally be subject to exit restriction measures.

IV. Criminal Procedure and Other Circumstances

In addition to the criminal circumstances set out in Article 28(1) of the Exit and Entry Administration Law, defendants or criminal suspects subject to bail pending trial or residential surveillance in criminal proceedings are also subject to corresponding exit restrictions, pursuant to Articles 71 and 77 of the Criminal Procedure Law.

Moreover, the areas of national security, counter-espionage, counter-terrorism, customs supervision, tax inspection, and combating telecommunications and internet fraud each have their own exit restriction provisions under the applicable laws or administrative regulations, all of which fall within the catch-all provision of Article 28(4) of the Exit and Entry Administration Law and must be applied on the basis of the relevant authorising norms.

V. Practical Risk Analysis for Foreign Business Personnel in China

(I) High-Risk Scenarios

For foreign business executives, the highest-risk scenarios are concentrated in two categories. The first is being identified as responsible personnel of an entity in enforcement proceedings. Even where the obligor is a corporate entity, a foreign legal representative, actual controller, or project handler may be individually subject to exit restrictions, and the personal exit restriction operates independently of the entity's legal personality. The second is the pre-litigation or litigation preservation stage. Unlike the enforcement stage, exit restrictions at the preservation stage do not require a judgment to have become legally effective; a creditor need only demonstrate a reasonable likelihood of success and the possibility that the respondent may evade litigation, and may then apply to the court to impose the measure.

(II) Operational Risks Requiring Particular Attention

In practice, some foreign executives who have incurred debts have attempted to avoid exit restrictions by changing the legal representative. Such approaches are generally not upheld by the courts. Relevant decisions of the Supreme People's Court and various higher people's courts indicate that the original legal representative at the time the debt was incurred may still be identified as directly responsible personnel whose conduct affects fulfilment of the debt; even if the business registration has been changed during litigation, this does not eliminate the risk of being subject to exit restrictions.

In addition, for persons subject to exit restrictions who hold passports, the court may lawfully seize their passport or request the passport-issuing authority to declare the passport void; for those who do not yet hold a passport, immigration management authorities may take corresponding border control measures. A person subject to exit restrictions who believes the restriction decision to be erroneous may, within ten days of receipt of the decision, apply to the next higher people's court for reconsideration; the original decision remains in force during the reconsideration period.

VI. On the Current Legal Effect of the 1987 Four-Ministry Regulations

The Several Provisions on the Question of Lawfully Restricting the Departure of Foreign Nationals and Chinese Citizens, jointly issued in 1987 by the Supreme People's Court, the Supreme People's Procuratorate, the Ministry of Public Security, and the Ministry of State Security, established the fundamental principle of applying exit restriction measures with caution. Specific principles included: matters that can be handled earlier should not be delayed until the time of departure; where matters can be resolved through other means, measures such as confiscating documents to restrict departure should not be adopted; and where it is genuinely necessary to prevent departure at a port of exit, this should be limited to an extremely small number of circumstances.

The principle of cautious application embodied in these provisions retains reference value at the level of institutional values. However, it should be noted that all the prior exit and entry administration laws cited in that document have been entirely superseded by the current Exit and Entry Administration Law effective since 2013. In practice, current laws and judicial interpretations should be the governing reference; the specific provisions of the 1987 regulations should not be cited independently as a standalone legal basis.

VII. Conclusion

Based on a synthesis of the current legal framework, the principal circumstances under which foreign nationals may be subject to exit restrictions in China and their correct legal bases may be summarised as follows:

1. Criminal suspects, defendants, or persons whose criminal sentence has not yet been fully served, pursuant to Article 28(1) of the Exit and Entry Administration Law and the relevant provisions of the Criminal Procedure Law.
2. Persons with unresolved civil cases where the people's court has decided to prohibit departure, pursuant to Article 28(2) of the Exit and Entry Administration Law.
3. Persons subject to enforcement who fail to fulfil obligations under legally effective instruments, as well as legal representatives, principal responsible persons, and directly responsible personnel of entities subject to enforcement, pursuant to Article 255 of the Civil Procedure Law and Articles 23 and 24 of the Supreme People's Court Interpretation on Enforcement Procedures.
4. Persons who have failed to pay wages, where a decision prohibiting departure has been issued by the relevant department of the State Council or the provincial-level people's government, pursuant to Article 28(3) of the Exit and Entry Administration Law.
5. Prevention of absconding abroad by persons under investigation in supervisory investigation proceedings, pursuant to Article 30 of the Law on Supervision and Article 162 of its Implementing Regulations.
6. Other circumstances where departure is prohibited under laws or administrative regulations, requiring explicit authorisation from a higher-level norm, pursuant to the catch-all provision of Article 28(4) of the Exit and Entry Administration Law.

For foreign business executives operating in China, the two categories of highest practical risk are identification as responsible personnel of an entity in civil and commercial enforcement proceedings, and exit restrictions at the pre-litigation or litigation preservation stage. It is recommended that relevant individuals pay close attention to these risks in the course of their China business activities and conduct preventive risk assessments with the assistance of legal counsel.
